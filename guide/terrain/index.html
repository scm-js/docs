Terrain

Terrain is painted with four brushes, each a tab in the palette:

Brush Paints
Isometric Terrain types, laying the cliffs and edges around them. The one to use for almost everything.
Rect Flat ground of one terrain type, with StarEdit's random variation. No cliffs, no edges.
Tile Any single tile by number: cliff pieces, doodad tiles, anything the tileset has.
Blend One tile next to a tile you picked, chosen from those whose facing edge continues it.

Drag to paint. [ and ] resize the brush from 1×1 to 7×7, Alt+click picks the terrain or tile under the cursor, and right-click offers Pick and Fill Area. The Properties panel breaks the tile under the cursor into group, slot, elevation, walkability and buildability.

The isometric brush#

The isometric brush is StarEdit's diamond brush. It sets the diamond under the cursor to a terrain and works outward from there: a neighbour that cannot border the new terrain becomes the terrain in between, and cliff faces stack as tall as the tileset draws them. A single click of high ground gives a small mesa; a wider brush gives flat high ground inside a cliff ring.

The brush works from the map's isometric record, a part of the file that only editors read. Protected maps often have it stripped; the Repair plugin rebuilds it as such a map opens, exactly where the ground was laid with the isometric brush and as a best guess elsewhere. The Isometric tab warns when the record and the ground disagree, and when part of the map is ground the brush cannot join up with, such as hand-placed tiles or another editor's work.

Rect, Tile and Blend#

  • Rect lays flat ground and nothing else, for filling an area you will shape afterwards, or for ground under a doodad.
  • Tile is for the pieces the isometric brush will not give you: a single cliff tile, a doodad's tile, a corner the cliff sets do not have.
  • Blend is for joins the cliff sets never had — dirt into a doodad's base, one edge set into another, a hand-laid shoreline. Click a tile to make it the anchor, and the palette lists for each side the tiles whose opposite edge continues it, best seam first.

These three leave the isometric record alone.

Overlays and tools#

Two overlays on the View menu show what the ground means to the game:

View ▸ Walkability (Ctrl+Shift+W) draws the ground as a unit walks it: islands, the areas the map divides into, the chokes between them with their widths, and the distances between start locations. In the editor at scmjs.dev its panel (Tools ▸ Walkability…) can also have the game itself walk the map: the regions the game plans routes over, real units walked between the start locations, a group of twelve units timed through every choke, and how fast each start location mines with 8, 16 and 24 workers.

File ▸ Import ▸ Terrain from Image… turns a picture into terrain with the isometric brush, over the whole map or a rectangle. Walkability and Terrain from Image are plugins, on from the start; see Plugins.

Tools ▸ Replace Terrain… turns every tile of one terrain type (or one exact tile) into another, over the whole map or the area marked on the Cut / Copy / Paste layer. Tools ▸ Fill Terrain lays the whole map anew. Both are one undo step.

Symmetry mirrors what you do across the map's axes or about its centre. M or the toolbar button turns it on and off, and the list beside the button picks the mode: left ↔ right, top ↔ bottom, four-way, a half turn, quarter turns, or either diagonal. The last three need a square map. While a mode is on, the axes show on the map and the ghost shows where the copies will land.

  • The terrain brushes (all but Blend), the fills and the fog brush also paint on the mirror images.
  • Placing a unit, sprite, doodad or location places one at each image as well. Each copy goes to its own player: P1's start location gives P2's under the two-way modes, and P2's, P3's and P4's under the four-way ones. Neutral units and resources stay neutral. A doodad that would have to turn is skipped.
  • Selecting something also selects whatever stands at its images. Dragging then moves them all as mirror images, resizing a location resizes its copies, and Delete removes them all. A map made symmetric by hand works the same way.

Each change is one undo step. Tools ▸ Symmetry… describes each mode and has the option to keep copies for the same player instead.

The Terrain palette's Remove stranded units tick deletes the ground units a stroke has just left where they cannot walk, which the game would remove at the start, as part of the same undo step. Buildings stay where they are: the game makes them on any ground.
